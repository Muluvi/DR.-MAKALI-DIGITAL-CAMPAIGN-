**Source pack:** `kitui-source-documents.pdf`, 170 pages (the uploaded merge with the Catholic University of Eastern Africa admissions brochure removed). **All page numbers below refer to this cleaned file.**
**Read against:** the proposal as published at https://dr-makali-digital-campaign.vercel.app/ (commit `4bfc078`). Section numbers (1.1 to 6.5, Annexes A to G) are the proposal's current numbers.
**Companion data:** `kitui-scorecard-projects.csv`, all 401 projects the county scorecard itemises, with cost, constituency and page.

## 2. The ten findings that matter most

1. **The proposal undercounts how many voters own a phone, and the error makes its strongest workstream look weaker.**
   - **What the pack shows:** 77.7% of men and 73.9% of women aged 18 and over own a mobile phone (KDHS 2022, p. 9).
   - **What the proposal uses:** 44.1%, which is a share of *all residents*, children included. Applied to the register, it puts **338,588 voters** in "no phone: radio or in person only".
   - **The corrected figure:** on the adult rate (calculated: 75.7% weighted by the register's sex split), about **459,000 of the 605,703 July 2026 voters own a phone, and about 147,000 do not.**
   - **Why it matters:** the SMS and USSD layer Firefly operates (Workstream 9) can reach roughly **1.7 times** the voters the proposal currently claims. → Section 3.1.
2. **The constituencies the proposal targets are also the county's hungriest.** NDMA ranks food-insecurity severity across the eight sub-counties (p. 121–122):
   - **Mwingi North 1st, Kitui South 2nd, Kitui East 3rd.** Kitui Central, his home constituency, is **8th (least severe)**.
   - **Hotspot wards** include Kyuso, Tseikuru, Ngomeni, Tharaka, Athi, Ikutha, Kanziko and Mutha: the same wards the proposal's reach effort is weighted towards.
   - **Why it matters:** the recognition strategy and the needs case now point to the same places, and the targeting can be defended on need, not only on electoral arithmetic. → Section 3.2.
3. **Women are 52% of Kitui's registered voters** (2022, p. 10). The proposal names no women's segment. The same sheet shows:
   - **60.2% of women aged 15–49 had no paid work in the last 12 months**, against 40.3% nationally (men: 21.9%);
   - women carry **five times the unpaid work** of men (14.8% of time against 2.8%);
   - women's internet use is **23.3%** against 34.7% for men. → Section 3.4.
4. **The incumbent's scorecard is the baseline for the verification pillar.**
   - **What it is:** 401 projects itemised with cost, ward and status, totalling **KSh 1.36 billion**, out of the "2,244 projects" it claims.
   - **Why it matters:** this is exactly the public register the proposal's service-delivery tracker is designed to check. The campaign does not have to build its list of promises from scratch; the county has published one. → Section 4.
5. **On the scorecard's own itemised list, spending per registered voter ranges from KSh 3,278 in Kitui Central to KSh 884 in Mwingi West** (calculated).
   - Mwingi West is in the proposal's target pool and receives the lowest spend per voter.
   - Kitui Central's figure is inflated by county-wide facilities at the Kitui County Referral Hospital. → Section 4.2.
6. **Water is the strongest theme, and the pack adds fresh, ward-named evidence** (pp. 103–104, 6, 116):
   - only 21.2% of residents reach drinking water in under 30 minutes, against 67.9% nationally;
   - 28% of water sources were not working in July 2025, and in **Kitui Central 44%** were not;
   - return trips to water exceed 7 km in nine named wards;
   - **2,244 school units have no safe water within 100 m.** → Section 3.3.
7. **Statistics the proposal marks "not yet tiered" can now be tiered or replaced.** These include education levels, cooking fuel, livestock numbers, disability and farm dependence. → Section 3.6.
8. **New policy gaps the proposal does not yet mention:**
   - **school meals reach a minority of schools**, and NDMA links the gap to absenteeism and dropout. The report's two counts conflict (p. 111, 113; see Section 3.3, Theme 6);
   - **a fodder deficit:** 21,734 bales held against 215,000 needed, with no commercial fodder business in the county (p. 97–98);
   - **aflatoxin:** 32% of maize and 75% of groundnut samples over the limit in 2024 (p. 96);
   - **border insecurity:** killings on the Kitui–Tana River border and herder clashes in Ngomeni and Tseikuru (p. 93). → Section 3.3.
9. **The scorecard contains claims that do not reconcile with its own tables or with official surveys.** Examples: 1.5 million livestock vaccinated against 226,491 itemised; infant mortality "47 to 13" against KDHS 2022's 23; two beneficiary counts larger than the county's population. These are questions for the tracker to ask, not attacks to make. → Section 4.3.
10. **The incumbent's slogan already claims "resources".** The scorecard runs "Rasilimali Zetu, Maisha Bora" ("Our Resources, Better Lives") on every page. The proposal's governing line, "Kitui has resources. What it has lacked is leadership that understands how to use them", now answers that slogan directly. Used deliberately, it is a sharp contrast; used carelessly during a Wiper nomination, it reads as an attack on a Wiper administration. → Section 3.5.

## 3. Upgrades by proposal section

### 3.1 Reach and channels (2.9.1, 2.9.3, 3.3.2, 4.5.9): correct the phone model

| Measure | Proposal now | Pack | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile phone ownership | 44.1% of residents (CA/KNBS 2023/24) | **Adults 18+: men 77.7%, women 73.9%** (Kenya 80.4 / 77.5) | KDHS 2022, Gender Data Sheet F2, p. 9 |
| Own *and use* a phone or bank account | n/a | Men 78.3%, women 73.7% | G5, p. 10 |
| Internet use, last 12 months | 26.2% of residents | **Ages 15–49: men 34.7%, women 23.3%** (Kenya 56.2 / 44.2) | E10, p. 9 |

**What to change:**
- Keep 44.1% and 26.2% as resident rates, but **model voter reach on adult rates.** Voters are adults; children are not on the register. The current `reach.nophone` (338,588) is roughly double what the adult rate supports (about 147,000).
- Restate the reach split in 3.3.2 and the ReachSplit visual as a range: resident-based (today's figures) and adult-based (KDHS 2022). Tier both, state both bases, and do not average them.
- **The argument gets stronger, not weaker.** "Digital alone cannot win" still holds, because adult internet use is under 35%. The new claim is that **SMS and USSD can reach roughly three in four voters**, which makes Workstream 9 the decisive layer by the proposal's own logic.
- **The gender gap in internet use** (23.3% of women against 34.7% of men) is a reason to run the women's message on the offline layer (SMS, voice notes, barazas), not on social media.

### 3.2 Geography of need (2.7, 2.8.3, 2.12, 3.3.1): the pool is where need is greatest

**NDMA sub-county food-insecurity ranking, July 2025** (1 = most severe; p. 121–122):

| Rank | Sub-county | In the proposal's pool? | Hotspot wards named |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mwingi North** | Yes | Ngomeni, Kyuso, Tseikuru, Tharaka |
| 2 | **Kitui South** | Yes | Athi, Ikutha, Kanziko/Simisi, Mutha |
| 3 | **Kitui East** | Beside it (no party flow, 2.7.2) | Zombe/Mwitika, Voo/Kyamatu, Endau/Malalani |
| 4 | Mwingi Central | Yes | Nguni, Nuu |
| 5 | Kitui West | No | Mutonguni, Kauwi |
| 6 | Kitui Rural | No | Kanyangi |
| 7 | Mwingi West | Yes | Kyome/Thaana, Nguutani, Kiomo/Kyethani |
| 8 | **Kitui Central** | No (home base) | Mulango, Kyangwithya West |

- NDMA names hotspot wards in every sub-county, the home base included, so **the ranking is the evidence, not hotspot status.** The three most severe sub-counties' hotspots include **Kyuso, Tseikuru and Athi, three of the twelve largest wards** (2.3). The proposal can say the Phase −1 weighting goes "where the voters are **and** where the need is most severe", citing NDMA.
- **County phase:** IPC 2 ("Stressed") with a worsening trend. The marginal mixed-farming zone (42% of the population) was projected to fall to **IPC 3 ("Crisis")** in Oct–Dec 2025 (p. 90, 120). This updates the NDMA classifications quoted in 2.8.1 and 3.2.4 (Theme 1) and Annex B.3.
- **Livelihood zones:** mixed farming covers 55% of the population, marginal mixed farming 42%, formal and unskilled employment 3%. Wealth groups are very poor 29%, poor 31%, middle 26% and better-off 14% (p. 92). This is a Tier 1 basis for the "rural agrarian" and "agro-pastoralist" segments in 3.4.2.
- **Use in 2.12 (the diagnosis):** add a line to "What supports it". The pool is where county service delivery is under the most visible strain, so a verification candidate has the most to show there.

### 3.3 The six campaign themes (3.2.4) and the segments (3.4.2): new evidence

**Theme 1, water** (the proposal's strongest theme):

| Fact | Value | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Population reaching drinking water in < 30 min | **21.2%** (Kenya 67.9%). Matches the proposal's "21%", now with an October 2025 county citation | 6 (B1) |
| Population spending > 30 min to reach water | **61.8%** (Kenya 8.7%) | 6 (B3) |
| Water sources operational, July 2025 | **450 of 630 (72%)**. Lowest: **Kitui Central 56%**, Kitui South 61% | 103 |
| Return distance to water, marginal mixed farming | **5–7 km** (normal 3–5); **over 7 km** in Nguni, Nuu, Mui, Tseikuru, Ngomeni, Tharaka, Athi, Kanziko, Mutha | 103 |
| Water use per person per day | 15–20 l (normal 20–25) in marginal mixed farming | 104 |
| Water-vending price where boreholes broke down | KSh 40–50 per 20 l (normal 20–30): Malalani, Koi, Kinanie, Voo/Kyamatu | 104 |
| School units without safe water within 100 m | **2,244** (797 pre-primary, 679 primary, 558 junior, 210 secondary) | 116 |
| Adult women as a share of those who collect household drinking water | 66.0% | 6 (B2) |

- **Message use:** "In July 2025 more than a quarter of Kitui's water points were not working. Dr. Mulu will publish the status of every one." This is a verification promise built on an NDMA figure.
- **Caution:** the 44% failure rate in **Kitui Central** is in his own constituency. Water points are county assets, not NG-CDF ones, but an opponent can still use the figure. Prepare the answer before using the number.
- **On the 2,244 coincidence:** the school-water figure is the same as the scorecard's "2,244 projects". It is a striking contrast, but the school figure counts school *units* by level, and several may share a compound. Use it with that wording, or not at all.

**Theme 2, economy and household income:**
- **Green grams are the cash crop.** They provide 40% of cash income in marginal mixed farming and 30% in mixed farming (p. 94–95). Long-rains 2025 production was **146,522 bags (90 kg), 58% above the five-year average**, while maize was **41% below** (p. 95). This supports Pillar B (ndengu value chain) with a Tier 1 source.
  - **Do not** use it to confirm the proposal's untiered "45,000 tonnes" ndengu output. The long rains give about 13,200 tonnes and supply only 40% of annual crop production. The annual figure still needs its own source.
- **Markets and prices** (p. 102): maize KSh 59/kg (5-year average 56); a medium goat KSh 5,969 (average 4,262). Most households are buying food rather than storing it, with stocks lasting about one month instead of 2–3 (p. 96).
- **Fodder gap** (p. 97–98): 21,734 bales held against **215,000 needed**; no commercial fodder or pasture business in the county. This is a ready youth-enterprise idea for Pillar D and the youth segment.
- **Aflatoxin** (p. 96): KEPHIS 2024 tests found **32% of maize and 75% of groundnut samples** above the safe limit. This gives a food-safety angle to the aggregation and cold-storage pledge.
- **Livestock** (p. 96, 100): the proposal's livestock figures (613,000 cattle, 2,000,000 goats, 4,000,000 poultry, 326,000 donkeys; poultry in over 90% of households) are confirmed, with the source named as *livestock data validation 2023*. Replace "as cited in the evidence pack" with this citation. Goat disease morbidity and mortality: CCPP 29% / 15%, PPR 15% / 8% (p. 100).
- **Women's work** (p. 9–10): **60.2% of women aged 15–49 had no paid work in the past year** (Kenya 40.3%). Of women with cash earnings, 4.7% decide alone how they are used, 67.3% decide jointly with their husbands, and 27.9% say their husband mainly decides (Kenya: 8.4% / 51.6% / 40.0%).

**Theme 5, health and nutrition:**
- **Health insurance: 11.7%** (Kenya 26.3%, p. 8). Matches the proposal's `health.insurance` and adds the October 2025 county source.
- **69.5% report a problem accessing healthcare** (Kenya 52.4%, p. 8). This is a stronger loss-aversion line than the insurance figure.
- **Facility deliveries: 79.5%** (Kenya 88.1%); births with a skilled provider 86.0% (p. 8).
- **Disease outbreaks, Jan–Jun 2025** (p. 106): a confirmed **measles outbreak in Ngomeni (112 cases)**; **typhoid up 52.2%** (1,696 to 2,564 cases); dysentery up 8.5%. Attributed to water contamination and poor sanitation, which links health back to water.
- **Child diet** (SMART survey 2023, p. 107–108): only 42.9% of children aged 6–23 months meet minimum dietary diversity, and 30.5% a minimum acceptable diet.
- **Infant mortality, KDHS 2022: 23 per 1,000; under-5 mortality: 27** (both better than Kenya's 32 and 41; p. 7). Hold these against the scorecard's "47 to 13" claim (Section 4.3).

**Theme 6, education:**
- **The "13.0% never attended school" line can now be tiered:** 9.7% of males and 15.1% of females have no education (KDHS 2022, population 6+, p. 8). Use these two figures and drop the untiered 13.0%. Note that the measure differs ("no education", age 6+).
- **School meals reach a minority of schools.** The NDMA report gives two counts that do not agree:
  - p. 111: **439 of 3,969 schools** had a meals programme, reaching 10,628 learners through in-kind meals, 206,561 through cash transfers and 99,492 through parent-supported schemes;
  - p. 113: 1,994 of 5,868 school units had one, all parent-supported.
  
  Quote only what both support: coverage is partial, much of it is parent-funded, and the assessment links the gap to absenteeism, dropout and transfers out of the county (p. 114). It is still a **new, concrete, costable commitment** the proposal does not yet make.
- **426 schools were damaged in March–April 2025** (107 pre-primary, 137 primary, 134 junior, 48 secondary), affecting about 9,900 learners (p. 111). Schools in several sub-counties also reported cross-border and resource conflicts, cattle rustling and threats disrupting learning (p. 111).
- **Secondary attendance favours girls** (net 51.6% against 40.8% for boys; parity index 1.27, p. 9). Boys' secondary attendance is the gap.
- **Literacy (15–49):** men 99.3%, women 94.8% (p. 8). The "low literacy" framing in 4.5.4 should rest on the no-education figures above, not on literacy.

**New theme to add, security and resource conflict:**
- NDMA reports **two people killed on the Kitui–Tana River border** (Endau/Malalani) and herders migrating in. It also reports **clashes between herder communities in Ngomeni and Tseikuru**, and hyenas and elephants preying on stock and crops near Tsavo East (p. 93).
- The scorecard claims eight border police stations, six of them complete (p. 165).
- The proposal's agro-pastoralist segment already lists insecurity as a core anxiety (3.4.2). **This is the Tier 1 evidence for it.** Frame it as resource and border security, never in community or ethnic terms (Charter clause 6).

**Population and inclusion (2.8.1, 4.5.4):**
- **Population projections (KNBS):** 1,258,954 in 2025 and 1,327,463 in 2030 (p. 6). These update "approximately 1.2 million by 2024 (Tier 2)" in 2.8.1.
- **Ages 15–24:** 276,060 in 2025 (p. 6).
  - **Do not use the 18–34 row:** its 2025 total (98,006) cannot be reconciled with its 2030 total (419,978); it is an error in the source.
- **Disability (15+, 2022):** men 7.5%, women 5.5% (p. 6). This gives a Tier 1 figure for the accessibility case in 4.5.4, which currently says only "a substantial constituency".
- **Clean cooking fuel: 6.3% of households** (Kenya 21.2%, 2022; p. 6). This is newer than the 2019 census figure (92.6% unclean) in 3.5.3. Use the 2022 figure, stated as "93.7% do not rely on clean fuels" or as the 6.3% itself.
- **Farm dependence:** the county scorecard says agriculture employs 66% of the population and sustains 87% of rural households (p. 141). This is the county government's own statement and **can replace the unverified "78.4%"** in Message Pillar 2, quoted as the county's claim (Tier 2).

### 3.5 Messaging (3.1, 3.5, 3.6): use the incumbent's own frame

- **The slogan contrast.** The county's line is *"Rasilimali Zetu, Maisha Bora"* ("Our Resources, Better Lives"); the proposal's is *"Kitui has resources. What it has lacked is leadership that understands how to use them — and the discipline to prove it did."* The second answers the first.
  - **Nomination window:** Malombe's administration is Wiper, and the nomination is a Wiper contest. Use the *verification* half ("publish whether it happened"), not the *deficit* half ("has lacked leadership"), until the ticket is settled.
  - **General election:** the full contrast is available.
- **New framing examples for 3.5.3** (each uses a Tier 1 fact from the pack):
  - *Loss aversion:* "More than a quarter of Kitui's water points were not working in July 2025 (NDMA). Every broken borehole is a longer walk for a woman in Mutha."
  - *Verification:* "The county says it drilled 57 boreholes. Dial `[shortcode]` and check the one nearest you."
  - *Anchoring:* "Seven in ten Kitui residents report a problem getting health care. Dr. Mulu will publish which clinics have medicines this week."
  - *Messenger effect:* a parent from a school running a parent-funded meals programme, on why the county should carry it.
  - *Social proof:* "Green grams beat the five-year average by 58% this season. Kitui farmers adapt; the county should help them sell."

**How to use them:**

## 5. The 2015 Statistical Abstract: historical baselines worth keeping

Use this only as history; it is more than ten years old.
- **CDF allocations by constituency** (KSh m, 2013/14 → 2014/15 provisional; p. 75). Kitui Central's NG-CDF grew from **76.7 to 110.3** and stands at **192.6** in FY2026/27 (proposal 2.10.1). This frames his stewardship of a growing fund. In 2014/15 Kitui Central's allocation ranked 5th of 8; Kitui South (137.3) and Mwingi North (133.2) were highest.
- **KDHS 2014 baseline** (p. 87): stunting 46% (Kenya 26%) and facility births 46%, against 25.1% (proposal) and 79.5% (p. 8) in 2022. This is real county-level progress over two administrations. Do not use it to attack the incumbent, and do not claim it for Dr. Mulu.
- **County spending, 2014/15** (p. 74): development spending of KSh 4,051m against an approved KSh 4,789m (85% absorption). This is a historical comparator for the "low development absorption" line in 1.1 and Annex B.2.
- **Data quality:** the abstract lists 25 elected MCAs (p. 27); Kitui has 40 wards. Do not cite its governance tables.
