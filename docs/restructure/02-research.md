# 02 — The conventions, and which of them apply here (Phase 2)

Every source below is cited with a URL. Each finding is tagged:

- **[EVIDENCE]** — empirical study, measured behaviour, or a documented institutional standard.
- **[CONVENTION]** — practitioner norm, widely taught and widely followed, but asserted rather
  than tested.

The honest headline: **the "answer first" rule is convention, not evidence.** The evidence base
runs one level below it — on reading behaviour, which shows most readers of a long digital
document never reach its end. Answer-first is the practitioner's inference from that, and it is
a reasonable one, but it has not been tested against a 49,660-word persuasive proposal read on
a phone. Nothing in the literature has. I say so plainly here so that Phase 3's verdict rests
on the reading-behaviour evidence and on this document's own cross-reference graph, not on an
appeal to Minto's authority.

---

## 1. Minto's Pyramid Principle and SCQA

**[CONVENTION]** Barbara Minto, a McKinsey consultant, codified the Pyramid Principle in the
1970s: start with the answer, do not build up to it. Supporting arguments group beneath the
answer; each level summarises the level below.
([ModelThinkers](https://modelthinkers.com/mental-model/minto-pyramid-scqa),
[Road to Offer](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/pyramid-principle))

**[CONVENTION]** SCQA — Situation (agreed context) → Complication (what changed, why act) →
Question (the issue) → Answer (the recommendation) — is Minto's introduction structure, designed
for contexts "where time is limited and decision-makers have to quickly grasp important
information."
([Think Insights](https://thinkinsights.net/strategy/scqa-logic),
[Management Consulted](https://managementconsulted.com/scqa-framework/),
[SlideModel](https://slidemodel.com/scqa-framework-guide/))

**The instruction that matters for this engagement** is Minto's process/product split: reason
bottom-up from evidence, communicate top-down from the conclusion. The order in which an analyst
*arrives* at a finding is not the order in which the finding should be *presented*. This is
exactly the tension the brief asks Phase 3 to resolve, and Minto's own answer is unambiguous.

**Limit:** Minto's canonical form is the memo and the deck — units of 1–30 pages. Neither Minto
nor her successors address a 49,660-word document. Applying the pyramid at 50,000 words means
applying it *recursively per section*, which is a different and less-tested proposition than
applying it to a memo.

## 2. Consulting proposal and pitch architecture

**[CONVENTION]** The standard consulting-proposal spine is: executive summary → problem, with
evidence → hypothesis-driven approach → scope → work plan by phase → staffing → fees. It moves
"from context to content to commitment."
([Slideworks](https://slideworks.io/resources/how-to-write-consulting-proposals-like-mckinsey),
[Medium — 7-step framework](https://medium.com/@dfso2222/mckinsey-style-proposal-a-7-step-framework-behind-the-pitch-dda7ba143abd))

**[CONVENTION]** Minimum contents: executive summary, scope of work, timeline, **success
metrics**, pricing — with client responsibilities and terms identified as the sections that
"make the biggest difference."
([Paperbell](https://paperbell.com/blog/consulting-proposal-template/))

**[CONVENTION]** McKinsey-style proposals for a mid-sized engagement run 8–15 pages.
([Slideworks](https://slideworks.io/resources/how-to-write-consulting-proposals-like-mckinsey))

Two things follow for Kitui 2027. First, **success metrics are a first-class proposal section in
the standard form, not a measurement appendix** — the convention puts them alongside scope and
pricing, which is precisely where this document does not put them. Second, the document is
roughly 20–30× the conventional length for its genre. That is not a defect to fix by cutting;
it is a signal that the document is doing two jobs at once (pitch *and* reference manual), which
Phase 3 must address structurally rather than editorially.

## 3. Political campaign plan structure

This is the most directly applicable literature, and the most useful.

**[EVIDENCE — documented institutional standard]** NDI's campaign planning methodology sets the
**vote goal first**: determine population, registered voters, expected turnout, votes needed to
win, and contacts required — *then* target voters by geography, demographics, values and issues.
Goal-setting and targeting are consecutive, interdependent steps, with the vote goal providing
"the foundation for all subsequent targeting decisions."
([NDI Political Campaign Planning Manual](https://www.ndi.org/sites/default/files/Afgh-campaign-planning-manual-ENG.pdf),
[NDI Module 9 — Campaign Planning](https://www.ndi.org/sites/default/files/Module%209_Campaign%20Planning_EN.pdf),
[NDI Module 3 — Research, Strategy and Targeting](https://www.ndi.org/sites/default/files/Module%203_Research,%20Strategy%20and%20Targeting_EN.pdf))

**[CONVENTION]** Written campaign plan templates converge on: political landscape → goal →
targeting → message → field / communications / digital → budget → compliance → timeline.
([NDI Campaign Plan Template](https://www.ndi.org/sites/default/files/Handout%2010%20-%20Campaign%20Plan%20Template.doc),
[Everytown campaign plan example](https://www.everytown.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Campaign-Plan-Example.pdf),
[NGP VAN](https://www.ngpvan.com/blog/political-campaign-plan/),
[TrainDems](https://traindems.org/resources/campaign-plan-template))

**This is the single most important finding in Phase 2.** The discipline's own canonical
structure is *objective-first*: the vote goal is step one, and everything downstream is
justified by reference to it. Kitui 2027 has the vote goal (§1.3.1, the ~200,000 threshold) but
files the campaign's own objectives at §8.1 and §9.1 — 41,000 words after the reader needed
them. The document is not merely violating a consulting convention; it is inverted relative to
the standard structure of its own genre.

**African / Kenyan practice.** Thin and mostly commercial. What is findable confirms the
document's channel premises rather than its architecture: bulk SMS as a near-zero-cost rural
contact channel, offline-first canvassing apps that sync when signal returns, radio-plus-SMS as
the working combination for reaching non-connected populations, and consent requirements under
the Data Protection Act 2019 and CA rules before political SMS.
([Celcom Africa](https://celcomafrica.com/blog/bulk-sms-for-political-campaigns-kenya-2027/),
[Africa's Voices Foundation](https://www.africasvoices.org/case-studies/deploying-interactive-radio-sms-approach-to-support-anti-corruption-campaign-in-kenya/))
**I found no African-specific convention for how a campaign strategy *document* should be
ordered.** The evidence base here is thin and I will not manufacture one.

## 4. Government and NGO strategy-paper conventions

**[EVIDENCE — documented institutional standard]** HM Treasury's Magenta Book and the UK
Government Analysis Function treat **Theory of Change as central**: an intervention's document
should make explicit the chain from inputs through activities and outputs to outcomes and
impact, with the causal assumptions stated.
([Magenta Book](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book/magenta-book-central-government-guidance-on-evaluation-html),
[Analysis Function ToC toolkit](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/the-analysis-function-theory-of-change-toolkit/))

**[CONVENTION, with a documented failure mode]** Commentary on UK industrial strategy notes that
strategy papers frequently promise a logic model as "work in progress" while the theory of
change — "the most important part of any strategy" — is actually absent.
([Paul Mason, How To Save Britain](https://htsf.substack.com/p/getting-uk-industrial-strategy-right))

Relevance is real but partial. A theory of change is an **explicit causal chain from activity to
outcome**, which is close to what the author's five-layer spine is reaching for. The difference:
a theory of change is normally presented as a *single artefact* — one diagram or one table —
rather than as the ordering principle of a 291-page document. That is a usable idea for Phase 4:
express the logical chain once, compactly and visibly, rather than making the reader traverse
50,000 words to reconstruct it.

## 5. Data-story structure in analytics reporting

**[CONVENTION]** The dominant taught structure is insight-first with layered support: "start
with the key insight and recommendation, then offer a second layer of supporting evidence for
skeptics"; open on the finding, then give background and demonstrate why it should be believed.
([Effective Data Storytelling](https://www.effectivedatastorytelling.com/post/data-storytelling-demystifying-narrative-structure-in-data-stories),
[Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/business-intelligence/data-storytelling),
[Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/glossary/data-storytelling))

**[CONVENTION]** The completeness test is the "so what": if the audience can respond
"interesting, but what do you want me to do with this?", the story is unfinished.
([Udemy Business](https://business.udemy.com/blog/data-storetelling-techniques/),
[SAP](https://www.sap.com/resources/what-is-data-storytelling))

Note what this convention actually prescribes: **two layers, not five.** Insight on top,
evidence beneath it, for the skeptic who wants to audit. It does not prescribe walking every
reader through the derivation. That is a direct input to the brief's two-layer question, and it
is convention converging with Minto from an independent direction.

## 6. Information architecture for long-form web documents

This is where actual measured evidence exists.

**[EVIDENCE]** NN/g has tracked web reading since the late 1990s: users read in an F-shaped
pattern — the first line most thoroughly, then scanning left margins. Scanning is the norm; users
want summaries and inverted-pyramid structure.
([NN/g — Concise, Scannable and Objective](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/),
[NN/g — Scrolling and Attention](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/))

**[EVIDENCE]** Position on the page predicts readership: the closer information sits to the top,
the higher the chance it is read. Readership data cited in industry analysis puts **75% of
readers below 10% scroll depth** on an article.
([Digital Content Next](https://digitalcontentnext.org/blog/2020/03/09/the-truth-about-scroll-and-swipe-the-real-value-of-mobiles-most-famous-features/))

**[EVIDENCE]** Screen reading shifts effort toward scanning, one-time and non-linear reading, and
away from sustained concentrated reading. Comprehension under scrolling holds up for literal
recall but **degrades specifically for questions requiring an inference** — the reader must hold
two facts together and combine them.
([ACM CHI — deep vs. skim reading on smartphones vs. desktop](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3544548.3581174),
[Harvey & Walker, QJEP 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1080/17470218.2017.1363258))

**[EVIDENCE — countervailing]** Pew found long-form reading survives on mobile: readers spend
more time on longer stories than short ones, and engagement expands to meet an in-depth piece.
([Pew Research Center, 2016](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2016/05/05/long-form-reading-shows-signs-of-life-in-our-mobile-news-world/))
This matters: length itself is not the enemy. **Unsignposted length is.**

**[EVIDENCE + stated limits]** Progressive disclosure improves three of usability's five
components — learnability, efficiency, error rate — and helps people prioritise. But NN/g is
explicit that "more empirical research is clearly required," and that the technique only works
if **everything users frequently need is disclosed up front**, with hidden content clearly
signposted.
([NN/g — Progressive Disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/),
[IxDF](https://ixdf.org/literature/topics/progressive-disclosure),
[The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/design/progressive-disclosure))

The inference-comprehension finding is the most operationally important result in this whole
phase, and it converts Fault 2 of the audit from a stylistic complaint into a measured risk —
see §8.

## 7. What applies to *this* document, and what does not

The reader: WPF campaign leadership — the candidate, his campaign manager, party officials —
on mobile, under time pressure, deciding whether to engage Firefly, with the nomination window
closing September 2026.

### Applies

| Convention | Why it applies here |
|---|---|
| **NDI vote-goal-first campaign plan structure** | The reader is a campaign professional. This is the structure they already have in their head. A proposal ordered against their own discipline's canonical order costs them effort at every transition. |
| **Minto's process/product split** | The author reasoned bottom-up correctly. The question is only whether to *present* that way. Minto's answer, and the genre's, is no. |
| **Consulting-proposal minimum contents, success metrics included** | The reader is deciding whether to buy. Scope (§7.1), metrics (§8.1/§9.1) and price (§9.2) are the deciding sections and are currently scattered across the final third. |
| **Inverted pyramid / F-pattern / scroll-depth evidence** | Mobile, time-pressured, 49,660 words. Position on the page is a readership predictor here more than anywhere. |
| **Two-layer data-story structure** | Directly answers the brief's two-layer question, from an independent tradition. |
| **Progressive disclosure, with NN/g's precondition** | The repo already implements it rule-driven in `lib/collapse-groups.ts`. The precondition — frequently-needed content stays visible — is the operative constraint, not the technique. |
| **Theory of Change as a compact artefact** | Gives the author's logical chain a home without making it the document's navigation. |

### Does not apply

| Convention | Why not |
|---|---|
| **"8–15 pages"** | The document is a pitch *and* the reference manual for a 12-month engagement. Cutting to deck length would destroy the auditability that is this proposal's differentiator. The answer is layering, not compression. |
| **Minto applied whole-document** | The pyramid was designed for memos and decks. At 49,660 words it must be applied recursively per section, which is an extrapolation, not a validated application. |
| **Theory of Change as document ordering** | Magenta Book governs *evaluation* documents for a technical audience. This is a persuasive proposal for a candidate under a deadline. |
| **Journalistic inverted pyramid applied to every paragraph** | Would require rewriting prose, which Ground Rule 1 forbids and which the engagement does not need. It applies at the level of *section order*, not sentence order. |
| **Generic "chunk it and add accordions"** | Fails NN/g's own precondition wherever the reader must compare two facts — see §8. |

## 8. The finding that constrains the redesign most

Comprehension under scrolling degrades **specifically for inference** — questions where the
reader must hold two facts together
([Harvey & Walker 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1080/17470218.2017.1363258);
[ACM CHI 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3544548.3581174)).

Audit Fault 2 is exactly an inference across two facts: the ~200,000-vote threshold (§1.3) and
the digital ceiling that leaves the campaign 125,549 votes short (§3.1). The reader must combine
them to see why the offline-first architecture is arithmetic rather than sentiment. They
currently sit ~20,000 words apart, on a phone, in a scrolling interface — the precise condition
under which the literature says inference fails.

Two consequences for Phase 4 and Phase 6:

1. **Co-location beats cross-reference.** Moving §3.1's ceiling calculation adjacent to §1.3's
   threshold is not tidiness; it removes a measured comprehension failure.
2. **Progressive disclosure must not hide either half of a trade-off.** Where the reader needs
   two facts side by side to judge — threshold against reachable population, budget tier against
   the statutory ceiling, KPI target against "not yet measured" baseline — collapsing one behind
   a control reintroduces the same inference cost the co-location was meant to remove. NN/g's own
   precondition ("disclose everything users frequently need up front") says the same thing.

## 9. Where the evidence base is thin — stated plainly

- **No study tests answer-first ordering on a 40,000+ word persuasive document.** Minto,
  consulting-proposal architecture and data-story structure are all convention. They agree with
  each other, and they agree with the reading-behaviour evidence, but agreement among
  practitioners is not measurement.
- **Progressive disclosure's evidence is partial and NN/g says so.**
- **No African or Kenyan convention for campaign-document architecture was findable.** The
  regional literature is about channels, not documents.
- **Pew cuts against the simplest reading of the scroll-depth data.** Long-form survives on
  mobile. The case for restructuring is therefore *not* "the document is too long" — it is that
  its ordering forces inference across distance. Length is not the fault; sequence is.
