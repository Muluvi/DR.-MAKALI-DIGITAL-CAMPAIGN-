# The number

The path to the governorship is arithmetic before it is anything else. Kitui cannot be treated
as one undifferentiated electorate; capital, impressions, SMS volume and organiser time have to
be allocated in proportion to registered voter density and structural leverage.

Everything that follows is built from the official 40-ward IEBC register.

## What the register says

The county holds **532,758 registered voters** across **40 county assembly wards** in **eight
constituencies**, served by 1,578 polling stations. A further 75 prison-registered voters bring
the certified total to 532,833.

The victory threshold is **approximately 200,000 votes**, derived from Governor Malombe's
certified 2022 winning total of 198,004. Historical countywide turnout runs at approximately
62%.

Put those together and the shape of the task is exact. At 62% turnout, roughly **330,310
ballots** are cast. Reaching 198,004 to 200,000 of them means capturing **60.0% to 60.5% of all
valid votes cast** — 37.2% to 37.5% of the entire registered electorate.

That is the number. Not a plurality, not a strong showing: three votes in every five that are
actually cast.

## The register is not evenly spread

A flat distribution across 40 wards would put 13,319 voters in each. The real register clusters
hard in urban corridors, agricultural belts and sub-county headquarters.

The twelve largest wards — 30% of the wards — hold **201,267 registered voters**, or 37.78% of
the county electorate. A candidate who dominates those twelve is engaging a voting population
equal to the entire 2022 winning threshold.

The top twenty wards hold 315,006 voters, 59.13% of the county. The smallest ten wards hold
96,415 between them — 18.10% of the register spread across a quarter of the wards and a vast
share of the county's transit cost.

## The finding that reorders everything

Mwingi North, Mwingi West and Mwingi Central together command **200,198 registered voters** —
more registered voters than the entire 2022 winning vote total.

Registration is not turnout. At the county's 62% baseline, the Mwingi bloc yields roughly
124,100 ballots, so total dominance across all three sub-counties still leaves the campaign
about 74,000 votes short.

Mwingi is therefore necessary and not sufficient. No path to 200,000 runs around it, and none
ends there.
