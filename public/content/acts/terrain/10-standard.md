# What we know, and how we know it

Every figure in this analysis carries three things: the institution that published it, the date
it was published, and the exact geography it describes. A number without all three is draft
data and does not inform a field allocation or a shilling of spend.

Beneath that sits a three-tier classification, and the tier governs what a number is allowed to
decide.

**Certified official records** — IEBC gazetted registers, KNBS census bulletins,
Auditor-General reports — are the unconditional baseline for target mathematics, ward quotas
and budgets. The 532,758-voter register, the 40 wards and the 198,004 winning baseline are
hardcoded into the operating model and change only on a gazetted addendum.

**Independent field research** — reputable public polls, commissioned survey waves, academic
demographic work — tracks direction, always with its confidence interval attached. The Mizani
Africa survey of 7 August 2026 is an empirical snapshot carrying ±2.53%, not a fact about the
future.

**Internal and unverified intelligence** — single-source field notes, campaign hearsay,
self-reported crowd counts — generates hypotheses and tracks leads. It never drives money. The
report that Wiper will nominate by poll rather than delegates sits here, watermarked as
single-source until a party communiqué moves it.

When two sources disagree, the tier hierarchy decides first. Where sources are tier-equivalent,
the analytical unit audits sample sizes, sampling frame, fieldwork dates and non-response bias.
Where electoral modelling is involved, the lower and more demanding assumption is adopted to
prevent complacency. Where a conflict cannot be reconciled, both numbers are published
side-by-side with their sources rather than averaged into a figure neither source supports.

Nothing is interpolated, smoothed or invented to close a gap between datasets.

## The gaps, named

Four things about this terrain are not known, and the analysis marks them rather than filling
them.

**Ward-level polling does not exist.** Published surveys report countywide aggregates and broad
regional estimates. Certified ward-by-ward cross-tabulations are not in the public domain, and
the campaign does not guess ward-level preference.

**The 2013 gubernatorial returns are absent** from the certified baseline archive, ward by ward
and in total.

**The 2017 ward-by-ward tallies are unretrieved.** Countywide certified totals are established
— Ngilu 169,990, Musila 114,827, Malombe 74,681 — but individual ward sheets remain unverified
pending physical retrieval from the IEBC registry.

**Ward-level broadband penetration is unmeasured.** Sub-county and countywide connectivity are
sourced; disaggregated ward-by-ward coverage remains an internal operational estimate.

Three further gaps sit on the audience side and are scheduled into the baseline survey: the
exact agro-pastoral electorate, the civil service registry by ward, and the proportion of the
diaspora still registered to vote in Kitui.

## Two more things worth knowing about this county

**The county is poor and thirsty.** 60.4% of residents live below the poverty line against a
national average of 45.2%. Roughly 400,000 depend on untreated surface water. Drought pressure
ran through 2025, the NDMA classified the county at Alert phase in January 2026, food security
sat at IPC Phase 2 in September 2025, and national flooding in March 2026 disrupted rural
transport on top of it. Poultry is kept by over 90% of households, which makes it the most
inclusive economic entry point any message can use.

**The prize is KSh 13.79 billion.** The approved FY2026/27 fiscal strategy paper puts the
county resource envelope at KSh 11.64 billion in equitable share, KSh 1.03 billion in
conditional grants and a revised own-source revenue target of KSh 1.339 billion. Against that
sits the audit record: KSh 670 million in unconfirmed cash balances, KSh 1.09 billion in
uncollected rates and rents, a KSh 621.5 million IFMIS variance and KSh 356.2 million in
unexplained inter-account transfers, with pending bills around KSh 1.3 billion and first-quarter
development absorption at 18% — though independent media reports alleged 5%.

An economist running on fiscal competence in a county with that audit record is not a
positioning exercise. It is the argument.
