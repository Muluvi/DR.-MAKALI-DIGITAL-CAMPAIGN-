# Three counties inside one county

Kitui is 30,430 square kilometres — Kenya's sixth-largest county — holding 1,136,187 people at
37 persons per square kilometre against a national average of 82. It is 95.2% rural. Its eight
constituencies and eighteen sub-counties have distinct economies, distinct infrastructure gaps
and distinct political histories, and messaging is calibrated to each.

## The anchor: Kitui Central and Kitui West

Roughly 223,970 residents, 58,724 households, about 19.7% of the county population, at the
county's highest densities — Kitui Central at 251 people per square kilometre, Kitui West at
170, Katulani at 146.

This is the home base and the highest-literacy zone: 77,764 registered voters across Township,
Kyangwithya West, Kyangwithya East, Mulango and Miambani. Messaging here runs on track record,
professional competence and national standing. It is the only zone where the campaign begins
with a structural advantage, and in a nomination decided by poll, consolidating it early is the
cheapest vote in the county.

## The north: Mwingi

Roughly 255,795 residents, 57,821 households, about 22.5% of the county — Mwingi Central at 95
people per square kilometre, then Kyuso, Mumoni and Tseikuru at 48 and below, arid and
livestock-weighted.

Balancing representation between "Kitui proper" and Mwingi is the live political question here.
Northern voters need assurance that devolved funds are distributed equitably per ward without
regional bias — a commitment this candidate can make more credibly than any rival, because he
can publish the allocation methodology and then be evaluated against it.

Nearly a quarter of the county, and the zone where a Kitui Central MP is structurally least
known. It carries disproportionate nomination-phase weighting for exactly that reason.

## The arid and resource belt: Kitui South and East

Roughly 416,001 residents, 89,070 households, about 36.6% of the county, at densities as low as
9 people per square kilometre in Ikutha and 12 in Mutitu. Mutomo carries the largest population
in the belt at 113,356; Migwani, at 125 per square kilometre, is the market-centre exception.

Vast, sparse, expensive to reach, and decisive.

## How the weight is distributed

A caveat before the table drives any spend. These three zones total 895,766 residents — 78.8%
of the county — and name twelve of eighteen sub-counties. The remaining six, about 240,000
residents, sit in no zone. The weightings below therefore distribute 100% of budget across
zones covering under four-fifths of the population. Those six sub-counties need either
assignment to a zone or an explicit statement of why they are out of scope.

| Zone | Share of population | Nomination phase | Phase 1–2 | GOTV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor — Central and West | 19.7% | 20% | 25% | 25% |
| Mwingi block | 22.5% | **35%** | 25% | 25% |
| Arid and resource belt | 36.6% | **30%** | 30% | 35% |
| Rotating / testing | — | 15% | 20% | 15% |

The nomination phase deliberately over-indexes on Mwingi and the arid belt relative to
population share, because the deficit being closed is a recognition deficit and it is
concentrated outside the home base. Weightings are reviewed monthly against tracking data and
reallocated.
