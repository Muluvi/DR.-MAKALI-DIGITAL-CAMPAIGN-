# The wards that decide it are the wards where he is unknown

Dr. Mulu has represented Kitui Central for thirteen consecutive years. Recognition is saturated
in the home constituency and in the adjacent wards of Kitui West and Kitui Rural — Matinyani,
Kauwi, Kisasi, Mbitini.

Outside that belt, three zones carry a steep deficit. The Mwingi sub-counties hold 200,198
registered voters, 37.58% of the register, and there the identity of "Kitui Central MP" limits
spontaneous recall against countywide office holders. Kitui South holds 75,372 voters, remote
across long transit corridors and shaped by its own leadership dynamics. The Kitui East border
wards — Endau/Malalani, Voo/Kyamatu, Mutitu/Kaliku — sit inside a constituency of 65,377.

## The overlap

The question that matters is whether the deficit wards and the decisive wards are the same
wards. They are, almost exactly.

Of the twelve largest wards in the county, five sit squarely in the deepest deficit territory:
Kyuso, Tseikuru, Mumoni, Athi and Ikanga/Kyatune — **83,496 registered voters** in five wards
where the candidate is least known and the register is heaviest.

Mwingi North alone contains three of the county's seven largest wards: Kyuso at 19,921,
Tseikuru at 16,471 and Mumoni at 15,877 — 52,269 voters in severe deficit. Kitui South
contributes Athi at 15,843 and Ikanga/Kyatune at 15,384, another 31,227. Mwingi Central adds
Central, Nguni and Nuu — a further 40,419 voters in deficit territory.

Taken together, Mwingi's 200,198 and Kitui South's 75,372 make a deficit pool of **275,570
registered voters: 51.72% of the entire county register.**

## What follows from it

More than half the register sits in the candidate's primary recognition deficit zones. Organic
spillover from Kitui Central will not reach it, and no amount of consolidation at home
substitutes for it. The allocation has to move.

Sixty-five per cent of nomination-phase digital advertising is geofenced exclusively to Mwingi
North, Mwingi Central, Mwingi West and Kitui South. Seventy per cent of the offline SMS and
USSD onboarding infrastructure, and 240 of the 400 ward captains, concentrate in those 24
northern and southern wards.

The message in those wards is not the message at home. It is countywide fiscal redistribution —
how an economist's control of public finance directs water and health capital into wards that
have historically been last in the queue: Kyuso, Tseikuru, Athi, Mutha.

Consolidating a base is cheaper than converting a stranger. But the base cannot get him to
200,000, and the strangers can.
