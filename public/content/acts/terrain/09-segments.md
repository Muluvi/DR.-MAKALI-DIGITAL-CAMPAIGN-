# Six electorates, not one

A generic countywide message fails for a structural reason. An offline smallholder in Tseikuru
weighing drought mitigation has different information channels, different daily anxieties and
different economic incentives from an urban bodaboda operator in Kitui Township or a diaspora
professional remitting money from Nairobi.

The register splits six ways. Each segment has a size, a channel, an anxiety and a lever.

## Rural smallholders — the agrarian core

**Roughly 507,000 registered voters**, derived by applying the county's 95.2% rural share to
the register. The register is not published by rural-urban split, so this is a derived estimate
and is treated as one.

They are spread across the agricultural belts of Kitui Central, Kitui West, Kitui Rural, Mwingi
Central, Mwingi West and Kitui East — 86.4% offline, feature-phone dominant, heavy evening
radio listeners, weekly market-goers.

Their anxiety is seasonal crop failure, unmitigated drought, exploitative middlemen for green
grams and pigeon peas, and erratic subsidised seed and fertiliser. The lever is a guaranteed
minimum return backed by county cold storage and aggregate marketing, plus decentralised
solar-powered sand dams and boreholes — with CDF-funded water harvesting tanks already
installed across Kitui Central schools and dispensaries as the proof it can be delivered.

Reached through Kikamba radio in the 06:00–08:30 and 19:00–21:30 windows, localised bulk SMS
through the ward captain network, and market barazas, church fellowships and SACCO meetings.

## Agro-pastoralists — the arid borderlands

**Roughly 80,000 to 105,000 registered voters**, estimated at 15–20% of the rural population.
Precise sizing requires primary household survey and is named as a gap rather than guessed.

Concentrated in Mwingi North, the northern wards of Mwingi Central, Kitui South and the Kitui
East borderlands. Dispersed geography, intermittent 2G, solar radio, pastoral migration routes.

Their anxiety is banditry along the Tana River and Garissa borders, pasture depletion, absent
livestock off-take during drought, disease outbreaks and predatory buyers. The lever is
county-financed vaccination corridors, abattoir capacity in Mwingi and Mutomo, and security
coordination with national government backed by parliamentary budget influence.

Reached through radio pastoralist segments, weekly livestock markets at Nguni, Tseikuru, Mutha
and Kanziko, and through clan elders, grazing committee chairs and trader association leaders.

## The youth cohort, 18 to 35

**Roughly 234,000 registered voters — about 44% of the register.** Clustered in Kitui Township,
the Kwa Vonza university belt, Mwingi Central town, Mutonguni and Kabati, and dispersed across
rural trading centres. Smartphone adoption inside this group runs above 70%.

Their anxiety is chronic un- and under-employment, predatory digital lending, arbitrary county
revenue enforcement against bodaboda riders and kiosks, and no capital to start anything. The
lever is dismantling the handout model in favour of an institutionalised County Youth
Enterprise and Innovation Fund, TVET and driving-school vouchers, and zero-rated informal trade
licences — with a transparent NG-CDF bursary formula that educated thousands without patronage
as the evidence.

Reached through Meta and TikTok short-form video, WhatsApp forwards, bodaboda stage
associations, football tournaments and the student unions at SEKU, KU Kitui and Kitui KMTC.

## Urban and peri-urban traders

**Roughly 26,000 registered voters**, derived from the 4.8% urban classification — again
derived, not measured. Messaging reach extends well past that classification, because the
peri-urban trading centres sit inside wards the census counts as rural.

Based in Kitui Township, Mwingi Central town, Kwa Vonza/Yatta, Mutomo, Matinyani and Nguutani.
Smartphone connectivity between 45% and 55%, constant WhatsApp business use, daily contact with
county revenue collectors.

Their anxiety is cess and taxes levied without market infrastructure, harassment by county
enforcement, and fires in markets with no engine response. The lever is a single unified
business permit, 24-hour solar-lit and secured markets, sanitation, and a predictable tax code.

Reached through business-district walk-throughs, trader associations, the KNCCI Kitui chapter,
geofenced Meta advertising and midday business talk radio.

## Professionals, civil servants and educators

**Roughly 25,000 to 35,000 registered voters** — teachers, health workers, county and national
civil servants, bank staff and clergy. Exact sizing requires administrative research and is
named as a gap.

Based in the administrative hubs and in public schools and hospitals across all forty wards.
Above 90% smartphone and laptop connectivity; active on X, Facebook, LinkedIn and professional
WhatsApp groups; readers of national dailies and policy analysis.

Their anxiety is delayed statutory deductions, stalled promotion, drug stockouts that undermine
clinical practice, and politicised public appointments. The lever is meritocratic
administration, on-time payment of county health workers, a professionalised County Public
Service Board, and governance free of cronyism — carried by the candidate's own academic and
professional standing.

Reached through policy whitepapers, LinkedIn and X briefs, regional and national television,
and union delegate barazas with KNUT, KUPPET, KMPDU and KEPSHA.

## The out-of-county diaspora

**More than 150,000 individuals**, concentrated in Nairobi, Mombasa and the Machakos-Makueni
urban centres. What is not known is how many remain registered to vote in Kitui, and that gap
is named rather than filled.

Their significance is not primarily their ballots. They pay school fees, medical bills and
funeral costs for rural relatives, and their political endorsements carry real weight inside
family decisions. Above 95% connected.

Their anxiety is inefficient county spending that keeps their parents and siblings in permanent
need of emergency bailouts. The lever is an economist who manages county funds with audited
transparency and builds the water and health systems that reduce the remittance burden.

Reached through geofenced advertising on Nairobi and Coast IP ranges, diaspora business
breakfasts and town halls, and organised professional and welfare WhatsApp forums.
