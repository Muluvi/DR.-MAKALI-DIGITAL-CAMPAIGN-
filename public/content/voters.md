## 11.1 The universes: base, persuasion, turnout

```lead
Segments say where to speak and in what language; universes say what success looks like there.
```

The segments in Section 11.2 describe **who** the voters are. The universes describe **what the campaign needs each group to do**, which is a different question and the one the objectives in Section 8.2 are written against. Segments say where to speak and in what language; universes say what success looks like there.

**Why two sizes are not stated.** No official source reports ward-level recognition or support (Section 7.3). This proposal works from existing records only: Week 1 measures what his own channels reach (Section 18.5), which is evidence about reach, not support, and the IEBC's ward-level results (Section 2.2) show how each ward has actually voted. A register count is the most a universe can contain, never what it does contain. Estimating the difference would be the exact failure the ban on guesswork in Annex A.1.3 forbids.

## 11.2 The six voter segments

```lead
**Each segment carries the content type that commissions for it** (Section 10.1), so the segmentation has an output rather than only a description.
```

**Each segment carries the content type that commissions for it** (Section 10.1), so the segmentation has an output rather than only a description. P4, *He came, and this is what he said he would do*, is not in the table because it is not segment-led: it is ward-led, run in the channels of whichever ward Dr. Makali Mulu visited that day, and it feeds P1 twelve weeks later.

Where an official indicator bears on a segment — drought and food security (Section 3.2), poverty and water (Sections 1.3 and 3.1), health (Section 3.3) — its message cites that indicator. The rest is tested only by how content performs on reach and engagement, and a hypothesis the weekly brief cannot support is dropped rather than asserted.

**A segment that runs across the six: women, the household economy and the water carriers.** Women are **52% of Kitui's registered voters** (2022). 60.2% of women aged 15–49 had no paid work in the past year, against 40.3% nationally and 21.9% of Kitui's men; women give 14.8% of their time to unpaid domestic and care work, against 2.8% for men; adult women are 66.0% of those who collect household drinking water; and 23.3% of women used the internet in the past year, against 34.7% of men (County Gender Data Sheet, October 2025, Tier 1). The sample messaging in Section 10.4 already assigns a message to women's cooperatives and table-banking groups; this makes them a segment. Lead content type: **P2**, one household, one income stream. Lead channels: SMS, WhatsApp voice notes, table-banking groups and church networks, because the internet gap makes social media the wrong channel for this message. Proof: the poultry and water commitments. The sheet's violence statistics stay out of campaign messaging unless the campaign adopts a specific policy, and its County Assembly chart is verified before it is quoted.

## 11.3 How large each segment is, and the evidence

```lead
**These six segments overlap, and the figures below are not additive.** **Check the base before quoting any percentage here.**
```

**These six segments overlap, and the figures below are not additive.** A 26-year-old smallholder in Mwingi sits in segments 1 and 3; a trader who keeps goats sits in 2 and 4. Where a size is stated it is a census share or a derived estimate labelled as one; where no source exists, no size is given. None is a share of a partition.

**Check the base before quoting any percentage here.** Two denominators are in play: the **register** (532,758 voters) and the **county population** (1,136,187 residents). "95.2% rural", for instance, is a population share; "~44% youth" is a share of the register. The figure states which applies to each row.
