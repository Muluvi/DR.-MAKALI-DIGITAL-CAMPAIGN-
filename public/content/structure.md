
Dr. Mulu already has a team that publishes every day. This section is about how three more people sit alongside it without displacing anyone.

The campaign runs a **Lean Core Delivery Model**: a tightly coordinated core steering team that owns campaign strategy, narrative discipline and data integrity, while **subcontracting specialised execution packages** (video production, audio jingles, media buying, USSD gateway infrastructure and event staging) to proven specialist vendors. Firefly joins that structure as its intelligence layer — it is not one of the execution packages, and it does not replace the publishing team.

## 14.1 Three teams, and the boundary between them

**Three cores, and they are not the same team.**

*   **The campaign's command structure** — the Campaign Manager and three functional leads in the
    chart above. Four roles. Firefly works to it and replaces no part of it.
*   **Dr. Mulu's existing digital team** — the people producing and publishing the daily record
    today. This engagement adds a brief to their week. It does not add a manager to their line.
*   **The 3-person Firefly core** named at every engagement level in Section 10.1 — strategy,
    analysis, and the offline layer.

Where this section says "four-person core" it means the campaign's; where Section 10.1 says
"3-person core" it means Firefly's; where it says "your team" it means the one already in post.

What the arrangement buys, stated without the usual claims about bloat:

1.  **Decisions in one place.** A single named campaign-side counterpart approves, and a single
    Firefly director briefs. Campaigns fail digitally when five people can commission content and
    no one can approve it.
2.  **Operational security by compartment.** External vendors work under NDAs that expose the
    specific creative brief, not the overarching strategy.
3.  **Capacity that scales without a standing department.** Surge roles activate against a named
    phase or KPI trigger and stand down again, so nothing is retained through a quiet fortnight
    simply because it was hired.
4.  **Specialist quality where it is measurable.** Vernacular recording and telecom infrastructure
    are bought from people who do only that.

## 14.2 A lean core with a defined surge

Firefly delivers this engagement through a small senior core team supplemented
by named surge roles activated at specific phases and budget tiers. This is
stated plainly because a campaign is entitled to know exactly who is doing the
work. A proposal claiming a large standing department it does not have will
fail on the first question about it.

| Role | Activated | Function |
|---|---|---|

| **Volunteer & Ward Champion Coordinator** | Phase 1 onward | Tiered volunteer programme (Section 8.11); ward champion training and reporting |
| **Crisis Communications Lead** | Phase 2 onward, or immediately on trigger | Rapid response, red-team drills, deepfake rebuttal protocol |
| **Earned Media / Press Officer** | Phase −1 onward (standard and premium levels) | Journalist relationships, debate clipping, radio placement (Section 8.7) |

**Community management and video editing are not on this list, and that is deliberate.** Both are
roles your team already fills. Adding them here would have been Firefly selling back capability the
campaign has.

**Surge triggers are explicit.** Each remaining surge role has a named activation condition — a
phase date, an engagement level, or a KPI threshold. Roles are not added speculatively.

## 14.7 How the existing team is assessed and upskilled

**Firefly assesses the team Dr. Mulu has. It does not replace it.**

**Week 1 — the capability map, not an appraisal.** Four axes, scored 1–4, with each person in the
room while it is scored: platform mechanics, Kikamba fluency and cultural register, measurement
literacy, and response speed under pressure. The map goes to Dr. Mulu and to the team at the same
time. Nobody is graded in private.

**Week 2 — one written standard.** The brief template, the caption pattern, the
commitment-and-date rule, the language rule, the escalation line, and the accessibility floor from
Section 8.5.3. Six pages. It belongs to the team from the day it is written.

**Weeks 3–8 — pairing, not training.** Firefly and the team write the first three briefs together.
Then Firefly writes and the team marks it up. Then the team drafts and Firefly reviews.

**Month 3 — the handover of the pen.** The team writes the weekly brief; Firefly reviews and
challenges it. That is the success measure, and it is stated here so it can be held against us.
An intelligence layer that makes itself permanently necessary has failed at the part that mattered.

**Where a gap cannot be closed by upskilling** — no native Kikamba producer, nobody who can cut
vertical video — Firefly names the gap in the capability map and the campaign decides whether to
fill it. Firefly does not fill it by default, and does not propose itself for the role.
