## 13.2 Who owns what: Firefly, the existing team, the campaign

```lead
**Firefly publishes nothing on Dr. Mulu's accounts and holds no credentials to them.** The only channel it operates directly is the offline layer it already holds: a WhatsApp Business API line and a USSD shortcode.
```

**The boundary.** The digital operation separates operational software, messaging infrastructure and data science, which Firefly provides, from voter mobilisation, ground staffing, broadcast advertising and logistics, which the campaign owns and runs. **Firefly publishes nothing on Dr. Mulu's accounts and holds no credentials to them.** The only channel it operates directly is the offline layer it already holds: a WhatsApp Business API line and a USSD shortcode. The access it needs to the owned accounts is read-only, analytics only, so the work can be measured. Everything else on the owned channels stays with the people who run it now.

**The Firefly proposition.** Firefly is proposed as the campaign's **intelligence and strategy layer**, not as a replacement for the team already publishing. The value sits above the output: an audit of what the existing channels are doing, a strategy derived from it, a weekly brief that turns the strategy into instructions, a measurement cycle that says whether it worked, and one operated channel, the offline SMS, USSD and WhatsApp layer, reaching the voters no owned account can.

## 17.1 Three teams, and how they fit

The campaign runs a lean core: a small steering team that owns campaign strategy, narrative discipline and data integrity, and **subcontracts specialised work** (video production, audio jingles, media buying, USSD gateway infrastructure and event staging) to specialist vendors. Firefly joins that structure as its intelligence layer — it is not one of the execution packages, and it does not replace the publishing team.

What the arrangement buys, stated without the usual claims about bloat:

1.  **Decisions in one place.** A single named campaign-side counterpart approves, and a single
    Firefly director briefs. Campaigns fail digitally when five people can commission content and
    no one can approve it.
2.  **Operational security by compartment.** External vendors work under NDAs that expose the
    specific creative brief, not the overarching strategy.
3.  **Capacity that scales without a standing department.** Surge roles activate against a named
    phase or KPI trigger and stand down again, so nothing is retained through a quiet fortnight
    simply because it was hired.
4.  **Specialist quality where it is measurable.** Vernacular recording and telecom infrastructure
    are bought from people who do only that.

**A lean core with a defined surge.** Firefly delivers this engagement through a small senior core team supplemented
by named surge roles activated at specific phases and engagement levels. This is
stated plainly because a campaign is entitled to know exactly who is doing the
work. A proposal claiming a large standing department it does not have will
fail on the first question about it.
