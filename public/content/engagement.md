## 13.2 Who owns what: your team, the campaign, and Firefly's platform

**The boundary.** The digital operation separates the platform, its hosting, upkeep and data checks, which Firefly provides, from analysis, strategy, messaging, voter mobilisation, ground staffing, broadcast advertising and logistics, which the campaign owns and runs. **Firefly publishes nothing on Dr. Mulu's accounts and holds no credentials to them.** It operates no channel at all: the offline layer, a WhatsApp Business API line and a USSD shortcode, is the campaign's to run. It needs no access to the owned accounts: the team enters their analytics on the platform, so the work can be measured. Everything on the owned channels stays with the people who run it now.

**The Firefly proposition.** Firefly provides the campaign's **data and analytics platform**, not a replacement for the team already publishing, and not an outside strategist. The team does the work on it: an audit of what the existing channels are doing, a strategy derived from it, a weekly brief that turns the strategy into instructions, a measurement cycle that says whether it worked, and the offline SMS, USSD and WhatsApp layer, reaching the voters no owned account can.

## 17.1 Three teams, and how they fit

The campaign runs a lean core: a small steering team that owns campaign strategy, narrative discipline and data integrity, and **subcontracts specialised work** (video production, audio jingles, media buying, USSD gateway infrastructure and event staging) to specialist vendors. Firefly joins that structure as its platform — it is not one of the execution packages, and it does not replace the publishing team or direct it.

What the arrangement buys, stated without the usual claims about bloat:

1.  **Decisions in one place.** A single named campaign-side counterpart approves, and a single
    strategy director briefs. Campaigns fail digitally when five people can commission content and
    no one can approve it.
2.  **Operational security by compartment.** External vendors work under NDAs that expose the
    specific creative brief, not the overarching strategy.
3.  **Capacity that scales without a standing department.** Surge roles activate against a named
    phase or KPI trigger and stand down again, so nothing is retained through a quiet fortnight
    simply because it was hired.
4.  **Specialist quality where it is measurable.** Vernacular recording and telecom infrastructure
    are bought from people who do only that.

**A lean core with a defined surge.** The campaign runs this operation through a small senior core team supplemented
by named surge roles activated at specific phases and engagement levels. This is
stated plainly because a campaign is entitled to know exactly who is doing the
work. A proposal claiming a large standing department it does not have will
fail on the first question about it.
