## 0.1 The ask

> **Firefly Management asks the campaign to approve a Phase −1 nomination sprint, and to meet within fourteen days to agree it.**

Three things are being approved, not one:

*   **The pivot.** Formal approval to make the Wiper nomination — not the general election — the campaign's primary digital objective, and to reallocate the digital, analytical and SMS infrastructure behind it.
*   **The deployment.** Authorisation to begin Phase −1: ward-level SMS micro-targeting, a visibility push in the sub-counties where Dr. Mulu is least known, and the first release of the public service-delivery tracker.
*   **The integration.** Agreement that the digital operation works inside the ground campaign's regional and youth volunteer networks rather than alongside them.

**Where the contest stands.** Dr. Mulu is at **22.1%** against a front-runner at **37.4%** — a **15.3-point** deficit that widened by 4.2 points between June and August 2026 (Mizani Africa, Tier 2; Section 3.1.5). The ticket is expected to be settled by countywide opinion poll in the final quarter of 2026 — reported, and **not confirmed by the party** (Tier 3; Section 3.1.2 sets out what would confirm it).

**What Firefly does not ask you to believe.** This proposal commits to a scope, a cadence and a standard. It does not commit to an electoral outcome, and no figure in it should be read as one (Section 2.4).

## 0.2 Why the answer cannot be "later"

*   **The ticket is the race.** In Kitui the Wiper nomination has been the decisive gateway to the governorship. Failing to secure it makes every downstream phase in Section 9.1 irrelevant, whatever its quality.
*   **The gap is opening, not closing.** Between June and August 2026 Dr. Mulu gained 1.9 points; the front-runner gained 6.1 — more than three times faster (Section 2.2). Each week of delay is a week of that compounding.
*   **The runway is already short.** Phase −1 was scoped against roughly 45 days of concentrated micro-targeting before the polling window opens in late October 2026. A decision on **30 September 2026** leaves about 25 of those days.

**The honest caveat, stated before the sell.** Firefly's diagnosis is that the deficit is a *geography-of-recognition* problem concentrated outside Kitui Central, not a credibility problem — and that this is the most solvable kind of deficit. That diagnosis has counter-evidence against it: Dr. Kasalu won the 2022 Woman Representative race with **201,899 votes**, more than the **198,004** that won the governorship the same year (Section 3.3.6). She has already demonstrated countywide vote-getting capacity above the winning threshold.

So Phase −1 tests the hypothesis in **Week 1**, through a baseline instrument and a message lab across all three zones, **before significant budget commits against it** (Section 9.1.1). If the recognition thesis is refuted, the response changes and the campaign finds out early rather than late. That test is not a formality; it is the first deliverable.

## 0.3 What is being bought, in one paragraph

Fourteen numbered workstreams with stated boundaries, across owned platforms, content, paid and earned media, ground integration, an offline SMS/USSD layer, volunteers, data, voter modelling, technology and analytics. Section 8.0 lists all fourteen on one page, together with **what is explicitly outside scope**. Three scope levels are defined in Section 10.1; **Firefly recommends Level 2 (Standard)**, because it is the level at which all 40 wards carry an active SMS and USSD layer — and reaching the arid belt is the whole argument. Commercial terms are settled separately and appear nowhere in this document.

## 0.4 What this asks of the campaign

Seven dependencies, set out in full in Section 15.1. Two of them gate everything else:

*   **A named counterpart** with authority to approve content.
*   **Appointment of the data-protection reviewer** (Section 12.5.5) — the long-lead item, needed in Phase −1, and the one that can delay the whole engagement if it slips.

The remaining five: approximately **3 hours** of candidate time per week; daily ground-team photo and video uploads; current ward-level registration data from the IEBC published file; access to the existing NG-CDF project record; and sign-off on the scope level so the team can be assembled.
