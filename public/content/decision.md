## 0.1 The ask

> **Firefly Management asks for one week and one export.**

Dr. Mulu is already in the conversation. A verified page, 745 posts, a team that publishes every
day in three languages. What the record does not yet show is a reason behind any single post: which
voter it was for, which of the **275,570** registered voters in his recognition-deficit wards it
reached, and whether it moved anything.

Firefly supplies that layer, in three steps:

*   **Analyse — Week 1.** Firefly audits the existing presence: ninety days of reach, engagement,
    language and geography on Dr. Mulu's own channels, read against the same window for Dr. Kasalu,
    Sen. Wambua and Hon. Ngilu. Public data for them; his own export for him. Section 1A specifies
    exactly what is measured and what would change the strategy.
*   **Strategise — Weeks 2–3.** From that audit, Firefly defines what to post, for whom, in which
    language, on which channel, and why. Four pillars, six segments, three languages, one calendar.
*   **Direct — Week 4 onward.** The existing team keeps publishing. Firefly issues the weekly brief,
    approves the calendar, and reviews performance against recognition in the sub-counties where
    Dr. Mulu is least known.

**Firefly publishes nothing on Dr. Mulu's accounts and holds no credentials to them.** The one thing
it operates directly is the offline layer it already holds: the WhatsApp Business API line and the
USSD shortcode `[CONFIRM/EDIT]`. Everything else on the owned channels stays where it is, with the
people who run it now.

**Where the contest stands.** Dr. Mulu is at **22.1%** against a front-runner at **37.4%** — a
**15.3-point** deficit that widened by 4.2 points between June and August 2026 (Mizani Africa,
Tier 2; Section 3.1.5). The ticket is expected to be settled by countywide opinion poll in the final
quarter of 2026 — reported, and **not confirmed by the party** (Tier 3; Section 3.1.2 sets out what
would confirm it).

**What Firefly does not ask you to believe.** This proposal commits to a method, a cadence and a
standard. It does not commit to an electoral outcome, and no figure in it should be read as one
(Section 2.4).

## 0.2 Why the answer cannot be "later"

*   **The ticket is the race.** In Kitui the Wiper nomination has been the decisive gateway to the
    governorship. Failing to secure it makes every downstream phase in Section 9.1 irrelevant,
    whatever its quality.
*   **The gap is opening, not closing.** Between June and August 2026 Dr. Mulu gained 1.9 points;
    the front-runner gained 6.1 — more than three times faster (Section 2.2). Each week of delay is
    a week of that compounding.
*   **The runway is set by the polling window, not by this document.** The window opens in late
    October 2026 on the reported timetable. The first directed cycle needs roughly six weeks in
    front of it, and the audit that sets its targets needs the first of those six.

**The honest caveat, stated before the sell.** Firefly's diagnosis is that the deficit is a
*geography-of-recognition* problem concentrated outside Kitui Central, not a credibility problem —
and that this is the most solvable kind of deficit. That diagnosis has counter-evidence against it:
Dr. Kasalu won the 2022 Woman Representative race with **201,899 votes**, more than the **198,004**
that won the governorship the same year (Section 3.3.6). She has already demonstrated countywide
vote-getting capacity above the winning threshold.

So Week 1 tests the hypothesis against Dr. Mulu's own data before anything is committed against it.
If his reach is already landing in Mwingi and failing to convert, the problem is message rather than
reach, and the response changes (Section 1A.4). That test is not a formality; it is the first
deliverable.

## 0.3 Who owns what

Fourteen numbered workstreams sit behind this engagement, and the change from the conventional
pitch is who holds each one:

*   **Six are Firefly's to operate** — the analysis, the modelling, the creative testing, the
    accessibility standard, the data layer, and the offline SMS/USSD/WhatsApp reach.
*   **Six the existing team already runs and will keep running**, to a Firefly brief — content
    production, publishing, community response, platform tactics, earned media, and the ground
    reporting loop.
*   **Two are not this engagement's to touch** — volunteer organising, and the technology stack
    Dr. Mulu already holds.

Section 8.0 sets out all fourteen with the owner named against each, together with **what is
explicitly outside scope**. Three engagement levels are defined in Section 10.1, and they differ in
the *depth of the intelligence*, not the volume of Firefly's output; **Firefly recommends Level 2
(Standard)**, because it is the level at which all 40 wards carry both reach reporting and an active
SMS and USSD layer — and reaching the arid belt is the whole argument. Commercial terms are settled
separately and appear nowhere in this document.

## 0.4 What this asks of the campaign

Seven dependencies, set out in full in Section 15.1. Three of them gate everything else:

*   **A named counterpart** with authority to approve content.
*   **Read-only analytics access** on the existing accounts — Analyst role, no publishing rights.
    Without it there is nothing to analyse, and the engagement cannot start.
*   **Appointment of the data-protection reviewer** (Section 12.5.5) — the long-lead item, and the
    one that can delay the offline layer if it slips.

The remaining four: approximately **3 hours** of candidate time per week; daily ground-team photo
and video uploads; current ward-level registration data from the IEBC published file; and access to
the existing NG-CDF project record.
