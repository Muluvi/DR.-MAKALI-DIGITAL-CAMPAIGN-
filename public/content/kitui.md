Kitui is large, rural, young and poor: **1,136,187** people on **30,430 km²**, a median age of **17**, and **55.2%** in poverty in 2021. Its next governor runs a resource envelope of **KSh 13.79 billion** a year. This chapter sets out who lives here and how the county is run; Chapter 3 sets out what they need.

## 1.1 The people: population, gender, age, households

The 2019 census counted **1,136,187** people: 549,003 men, 587,151 women and 33 intersex people (Tier 1, KNBS). KNBS projects **1,258,954** residents in 2025 and **1,327,463** in 2030 (Tier 1, County Gender Data Sheet, October 2025).

## 1.3 Living standards: poverty, water, electricity, phones and internet

More than half of Kitui lives in poverty, fewer than two households in five have safe drinking water, and fewer than one in five has electricity.

**Poverty.** The KNBS Kenya Poverty Report puts **55.2%** of Kitui residents, about 637,000 people, in poverty in 2021 (Tier 1). NDMA's 2025 long-rains assessment cites 60.4% "below the poverty line (KNBS 2019)" and a county poverty rate of 63.1%, on bases it does not reconcile with this one (Tier 2), so neither is used in its place. Stats Kenya reports 58.3% for 2022 (Tier 3); it is not used until checked against the KNBS 2022 poverty report.

**Water.** **38.5%** of households had safe drinking water in 2019, and **5.1%** piped water (Tier 2, KNBS 2019 via Stats Kenya). This is the census definition of a safe source; Section 3.1 sets out the other measures, which count different things. About **400,000** residents rely on untreated surface water as their main domestic source (Tier 1).

**Electricity.** **17.2%** of households had electricity in 2019, up from **4.8%** in 2009 (Tier 2, KNBS 2019 via Stats Kenya). No verified national comparison is available yet; the record that would settle it is the KNBS 2019 Census Volume IV household table.

**Phones and internet.** **26.2%** of residents use the internet and **44.1%** own a mobile phone (Tier 1, CA/KNBS 2023/24), so **73.8%** are offline. Chapter 4 sets out what each channel can reach.

**What it means for the campaign.** Most voters are poor, offline and without piped water, so the campaign is fought on services and reaches most voters off the internet.

## 1.4 How the county is run: 8 constituencies, 40 wards, sub-counties and villages

Kitui has eight constituencies, 40 county assembly wards and 18 administrative sub-counties (Tier 1, IEBC and KNBS). The county also counts villages as administrative units. No verified figure is available yet for their number; the record that would settle it is the Kitui County Integrated Development Plan 2023–2027. The reported count is of villages, not sub-locations, and the two are not used interchangeably.

Constituencies and wards are the electoral units, and the ward register in Annex B uses them. Sub-counties are the administrative units the census reports by, and they do not map one-to-one onto constituencies (Section 1.2).

The Governor of Kitui heads the county executive. Under the County Governments Act the office runs public services for more than one million residents. Its main responsibilities:

## 1.5 The county's money: the budget envelope, the development plan, the audit record


**The budget envelope.** The county's Fiscal Strategy Paper for FY2026/27 sets a resource envelope of **KSh 13.79 billion**; the total is cited through secondary reporting of the Paper (Tier 3) until the Paper is cited directly. The figures here are the Fiscal Strategy Paper's (the CFSP), not the Annual Development Plan's (the ADP), which is a different document. An earlier draft gave an ADP envelope of KSh 14.27 billion; it is not confirmed and is not used.

*   **Equitable share:** KSh 11.64 billion (84.3% of the envelope) (Tier 1).
*   **Own-source revenue:** KSh 1.12 billion in the Fiscal Strategy Paper (8.1%), raised to **KSh 1.339 billion** by the County Assembly. The approved FY2026/27 budget settles which applies; until it is in hand, both are shown (Tier 3, verify).
*   **Conditional grants:** KSh 1.04 billion (7.5%) (Tier 1).

The three parts sum to KSh 13.80 billion against a published total of 13.79. The KSh 0.01 billion difference is rounding in the source, and is shown as its own segment rather than absorbed.

**The budget itself was not approved on time.** The FY2026/27 county budget was still unapproved after 30 June 2026 (Tier 2, The Star, 6 July 2026). Once the approved budget is in hand, its figures replace the Fiscal Strategy Paper's here.

**The development plan.** The county's priorities for the term are set in the Kitui County Integrated Development Plan 2023–2027, held in the KIPPRA repository. No verified figure is available yet for its number of priority sectors: an earlier draft said 16, led by agriculture, water, health, education and roads, and the record that would settle it is the plan itself.

**The audit record.** The Auditor-General's FY2023/24 report on the county executive raises four findings: unconfirmed cash balances, uncollected rates and rents, an IFMIS variance and unexplained inter-account transfers (Tier 1). The Controller of Budget records pending bills and a development absorption rate of **18%** in the first quarter of FY2025/26 (Tier 1). Section 3.7 sets out the figures and the incumbent's own scorecard.

**What it means for the campaign.** A KSh 13.79 billion envelope is the scale of the job: the campaign translates it into ward-level policy targets, so that an economist's case is made in shillings per ward.
