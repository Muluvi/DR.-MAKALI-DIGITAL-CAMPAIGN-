## 1. Executive Summary

Kitui County stands at a crossroads. The next Governor will either deepen the
county's economic transformation or preside over another five years of
unfulfilled promises. Hon. Dr. Makali Mulu represents a rare convergence of
rigorous economic training, national legislative experience, monitoring and
evaluation expertise, and an unbroken bond with the people of Kitui.

Yet credentials alone will not win the seat — and, more urgently than the
existing planning cycle assumes, credentials alone will not win the **party
ticket**.

### 1.1 The immediate contest is the nomination, not the general election

Most digital campaign proposals for the 2027 cycle are written for August 2027.
This one is not, because the decisive contest for Dr. Mulu is happening now.

The Wiper Democratic Movement has opted to select its Kitui gubernatorial
flagbearer by **opinion poll rather than competitive primaries**, placing Dr.
Mulu, Woman Representative Dr. Irene Kasalu and Senator Enoch Wambua in a
measured popularity contest. Party strategists have indicated they want the
exercise concluded before the final quarter of 2026.

The published trend is not favourable, and it is moving in the wrong direction:

| Survey | Kasalu | **Mulu** | Ngilu | Wambua | Dr. Mulu's deficit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mizani Africa, June 2026 | 31.3% | **20.2%** | — | 16.3% | **−11.1 points** |
| Mizani Africa, 7 August 2026 | 37.4% | **22.1%** | 17.0% | 14.3% | **−15.3 points** |

Dr. Mulu's own support rose by 1.9 points over the period. The problem is not
collapse — it is that a rival is growing more than three times faster. In a
county where the party ticket is widely treated as the gateway to the
Governor's office, a nomination decided on measured name recognition and
countywide visibility is precisely the contest a disciplined digital operation
exists to win.

**This proposal therefore opens with a Phase −1: a concentrated visibility and
preference-shift operation aimed at the Wiper nomination window, with the
August 2027 general election as the second act.** Every subsequent phase is
preserved and strengthened, but the sequencing has been corrected.

### 1.2 What this proposal covers

A comprehensive, remotely executed digital campaign operation spanning social
media management, content creation in English, Kiswahili and Kikamba, online
fundraising, data analytics, website management, digital advertising, an
SMS/USSD layer for voters who are not online, earned media and debate
preparation, campaign cybersecurity, and a published data-ethics regime.

The central narrative is unchanged and remains correct: **Dr. Makali Mulu is
the Economist Governor Kitui needs.** His PhD in Economics and his record on
the National Assembly's Budget and Appropriations Committee position him
uniquely to diagnose Kitui's fiscal weaknesses, reorder county spending, and
unlock resources for water, agriculture, healthcare and jobs.

But the research behind this proposal surfaced something the original framing
under-uses. Parliament's own record lists Dr. Mulu as a consultant on project
management, strategic planning and **monitoring and evaluation**, a member of
the Evaluation Society of Kenya, and an **M&E Champion awarded by the Ministry
of Finance**, following earlier service with the United Nations in project
management and financial advisory roles. Kitui Central was certified the best
evaluated constituency in the Eastern region for FY2014/15.

He is not only an economist. He is a specialist in **measuring whether public
money actually delivered anything** — which is, precisely, the thing Kitui
voters have most reason to doubt. This proposal builds a public,
constituent-facing service-delivery tracker around that credential (Section
19B), turning a line on a CV into a daily demonstration.

### 1.3 What separates this from a standard social media plan

This proposal integrates predictive voter modelling, behavioural science
message framing, AI-assisted creative optimisation, gamified volunteer
mobilisation, a remote digital war room with scheduled red-team simulation,
multi-touch attribution, competitive intelligence, campaign cybersecurity
including deepfake rapid-rebuttal, accessibility compliance, and a published
Digital Ethics & Data Charter — all adapted specifically for Kitui County's
political, cultural, connectivity and regulatory realities.

Three of those realities shape everything that follows:

1. **Roughly 86% of Kitui residents are outside the internet-using
   population.** A digital strategy that only reaches the connected minority
   will lose a county that is 95% rural. Section 9B addresses this directly.
2. **The regulatory ground shifted on 7 August 2026,** when the IEBC gazetted
   the Election Campaign Financing Regulations, 2026 together with binding
   contribution and spending limits. Digital spend is now regulated,
   reportable, and criminally sanctionable if misreported.
3. **Kamba-language radio — the dominant channel for voters who are offline —
   is politically compromised.** Two of the most significant stations are
   associated with a direct rival in this race, and a third with the party
   leadership. Section 17A routes around this rather than pretending it away.

### 1.4 Why a remote operation is a strength

A remote digital strategist is not a limitation — it is a force multiplier.
Digital campaigns are won through speed, data and creative discipline, not
physical proximity. While the ground team knocks on doors and organises
barazas, Firefly runs the digital command centre: producing daily multilingual
content, targeting voters ward by ward, countering misinformation in real time,
and converting online attention into registered voters, volunteers and donors.

The strategy that follows is built for one purpose: to make Dr. Makali Mulu the
unavoidable choice — first for the Wiper ticket, then for Governor of Kitui
County.

## 2. Strategic Context: Dr. Makali Mulu and Kitui County

### 2.1 Candidate Profile

Hon. Dr. Benson Makali Mulu is the long-serving Member of Parliament for Kitui
Central and holds a PhD in Economics. In Parliament, he has established himself
as one of the country's most consistent and respected voices on public finance,
budget oversight and economic policy. His work on the Budget and Appropriations
Committee gives him direct, practical knowledge of how national resources are
allocated — and how county governments can better use their own.

**Assets the digital campaign will project daily:**

| Asset | Evidence | Digital application |
|---|---|---|
| Economic expertise | PhD (Economics); Budget & Appropriations Committee | Weekly budget explainers; "Dr. Mulu Explains" video series |
| **M&E specialism** | Consultant in project management, strategic planning and M&E; member, Evaluation Society of Kenya; **M&E Champion, Ministry of Finance** | The public service-delivery tracker (Section 19B) |
| International experience | United Nations — project management and financial advisory roles | Diaspora and professional-class credibility |
| Delivery record | KSh47m in bursaries to 12,573 students; **Kitui Central certified best evaluated constituency in the Eastern region, FY2014/15** — first of 71 constituencies in its category | Ward-level infographic proof points |
| Longevity and trust | 17 years in politics, 13 as MP for Kitui Central; CBS | "Steady hands" framing against newer entrants |
| Existing digital footprint | Active Kitui Central NG-CDF web presence | Phase 0 audit asset — do not rebuild what works |

### 2.2 The Competitive Field

Kitui's 2027 gubernatorial race is among the most crowded in the country. The
digital strategy must be calibrated to a two-stage contest.

**Stage one — the Wiper ticket:**

| Contender | Position | Digital-relevant strength | Exploitable gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dr. Irene Kasalu** | Woman Representative | Countywide constituency by design; sustained grassroots touring ("Ziara Mashinani"); currently leading on measured preference | Grassroots visibility is not the same as fiscal competence; her critique of the incumbent's equal-ward funding model invites a technical rebuttal Dr. Mulu is uniquely qualified to make |
| **Sen. Enoch Wambua** | Senator | Senior Wiper standing; steady party base | Trailing and losing share between the two published surveys |
| **Dr. Makali Mulu** | MP, Kitui Central | Deepest fiscal and M&E credentials in the field | Constituency-bounded name recognition — a Kitui Central MP is structurally less visible in Mwingi and the arid south than a Woman Rep |

**The strategic reading:** Dr. Mulu's deficit is a **geography-of-recognition
problem, not a credibility problem**. That is the most solvable category of
deficit there is, and it is solvable fastest by digital and SMS reach into the
sub-counties where he is least known. Phase −1 is built on exactly this
hypothesis, and Section 19A specifies the qualitative research that will
confirm or refute it before significant budget is committed.

**Stage two — the general election.** Beyond the Wiper field: former Governor
Charity Ngilu (NARC), former Senator David Musila, former Deputy Governor and
ex-Cabinet Secretary Peninah Malonza, Safaricom executive Nicholas Mulila, and
governance activist Francis Musili Kauta. The status of incumbent Governor
Julius Malombe remains subject to legal determination regarding his interrupted
terms and should be treated as unresolved in all planning.

### 2.3 The 2022 Baseline — What Winning Actually Requires

| Metric | 2022 actual |
|---|---|
| Registered voters, Kitui County | **532,758** |
| Polling stations | 1,578 |
| Winning candidate (Malombe, Wiper) | **198,004 votes** |
| Runner-up (Musila, Jubilee) | 114,606 |
| Third (Mueke, UDA) | 10,639 |
| Approximate turnout | ~62% |

**A winning number in Kitui is approximately 200,000 votes.** Every KPI in
Section 20 is anchored to that figure rather than to platform vanity metrics.
The relevant question is never "how many followers" — it is "what share of
200,000 has this operation touched, persuaded, and turned out."

The register is also growing. Nationally, the IEBC targets **28.5 million
registered voters by 2027**, up from 22.1 million in 2022, and reported
**2,936,516 new registrations between 29 September 2025 and 20 August 2026**.
Enhanced Continuous Voter Registration Phase I alone added 2,345,476 voters
between 30 March and 28 April 2026. The register will undergo a forensic audit
by an independent firm before certification.

**Verified Ward-Level Baseline (2022 Register):**
The verified 2022 ward-level register is fully integrated as our primary planning dataset. Key highlights include the largest wards (Kyuso at 19,921 voters and Township at 19,538 voters) and the smallest (Tharaka at 7,429 voters). The complete ward-level register of all 40 wards across the eight constituencies is the quantitative basis for all micro-targeting and SMS operations.

### 2.4 The Governorship Mandate

The Governor of Kitui County is responsible for delivering tangible
improvements in the lives of over one million residents. Core functions:

- Agriculture, irrigation and food security
- County health services and sanitation
- Water supply and drought resilience
- Early childhood education and vocational training
- County roads and market infrastructure
- Trade development and access to credit
- County budget formulation and accountable execution

**The budget he is asking to run.** Kitui County's approved Fiscal Strategy
Paper for FY2026/27 sets a total resource envelope of **KSh13.79 billion**,
comprising roughly KSh11.64 billion in equitable share (approximately 84.5% of
revenue), around KSh1.03 billion in grants, and **own-source revenue revised
upward to KSh1.339 billion**.

This figure is the single most useful number in the campaign. It is concrete,
official, and almost entirely unknown to ordinary voters. Every digital message
about fiscal competence can be anchored to it: *this is the money; here is
where it goes; here is what an economist would do differently.*

### 2.5 Kitui's Demographic, Economic and Digital Landscape

Kitui County is vast and sparsely populated. The 2019 Kenya Population and
Housing Census provides the demographic foundation:

- **Total population:** 1,136,187 (Male 549,003; Female 587,151; Intersex 33).
  Estimates place the county at approximately 1.2 million by 2024.
- **Total households:** 262,942 · **Average household size:** 4.3 persons
- **Land area:** 30,430 km² — Kenya's sixth-largest county
- **Population density:** 37 persons/km² against a national average of 82
- **Rural/urban split:** 95.2% rural (1,082,168) vs 4.8% urban (54,019)

**Poverty and vulnerability:**
- **60.4% of Kitui residents live below the poverty line, against 45.2%
  nationally** (KNBS, via the NDMA Kitui 2025 Long Rains Food and Nutrition
  Security Assessment).
- **Approximately 400,000 people rely on surface water** as their main drinking
  source.
- Livestock economy: approximately 613,000 cattle, 2,000,000 goats, 4,000,000
  poultry and 326,000 donkeys. **Poultry is kept by over 90% of households** and
  is gender-inclusive — an underused entry point for women-focused messaging.
- Climate volatility now runs both directions: the county experienced drought
  pressure through 2025 and was affected by the **March 2026 national floods**.
  Messaging should speak to *climate resilience*, not drought alone.

**Connectivity — the defining strategic constraint:**

| Indicator | Kitui | National context |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile phone ownership | 452,948 (42.9% of population aged 3+) | 84.1m active SIMs nationally; 157.7% penetration (CA, Q3 FY2025/26) |
| Internet usage | 143,340 (**13.6%** of population aged 3+) | 62.6m mobile broadband subscriptions nationally |
| Device mix | Feature phones remain widespread | Smartphones = 63.7% of connected devices nationally |
| Mobile money | Near-universal reliance | 53.4m subscriptions; 100.1% penetration; **602,470 registered agents** (March 2026) |

**The number that should govern the entire media plan: roughly 86% of Kitui
residents are outside the internet-using population.** A campaign that spends
its budget where it is easiest to measure will systematically under-serve the
wards where Dr. Mulu is least known — which are, not coincidentally, the wards
where his deficit against Dr. Kasalu is likely largest.

The mobile-money agent network is the underused asset here. Agents grew from
480,216 in September 2025 to **602,470 by March 2026** — roughly four times
faster than subscriptions. These are trusted, ubiquitous, commercially
motivated local touchpoints in exactly the settlements where broadband is not.

**Language.** Kikamba is dominant, Kiswahili widely understood, English used by
younger and professional audiences and by national media. This is the basis for

## 3. Digital Campaign Objectives

Six measurable objectives across two stages, from August 2026 to August 2027.

**Stage one — nomination window (immediate):**

1. **Close the recognition gap.** Move Dr. Mulu's measured preference in
   published county surveys, with a specific focus on Mwingi and the arid
   southern belt where his constituency-bounded name recognition is weakest.
   *Primary metric: published survey share and internal tracking (Section 19A).*

**Stage two — general election:**

2. **Visibility and recognition.** Establish Dr. Mulu as the most visible
   gubernatorial candidate in Kitui online — reach, share of voice, follower
   growth.
3. **Community building.** Build an active digital community across all 40
   wards and among the Kitui diaspora.
4. **Issue ownership.** Position Dr. Mulu as the authoritative voice on water
   security, economic transformation, youth employment and fiscal
   accountability.
5. **Voter mobilisation.** Convert online and SMS support into concrete
   actions: voter registration, pledges to vote, volunteer sign-ups, turnout.
6. **Reputation defence.** Monitor and counter misinformation, negative
   narratives, manipulated media and opposition attacks in real time.

Every objective is expressed against the ~200,000-vote win threshold
established in Section 2.3, not against platform-native metrics alone.

## 4. Core Campaign Narrative: The "Economist Governor"

Kitui faces severe socio-economic bottlenecks — chronic water scarcity, erratic
agricultural yields, 60.4% poverty, and the need for equitable resource
allocation across 40 wards and 30,430 square kilometres. Dr. Mulu's most
significant competitive advantage is his background in economics and his tenure
on the National Assembly's Budget and Appropriations Committee.

The messaging must pivot from standard political rhetoric to **competent
resource management**. The narrative frames him not as a politician who
promises, but as an economist who can show his working.

**The narrative has two halves, and the campaign has historically used only
one.**

- **The economist half:** he understands how KSh13.79 billion should be
  allocated.
- **The evaluator half:** he is professionally qualified to verify whether it
  *was* — and Kitui Central's certification as the best evaluated constituency
  in the Eastern region is documented proof he has done this before.

The second half is the more powerful, because Kitui voters have heard budget
promises before. What they have not been offered is a candidate whose
professional discipline is *checking*. Every digital asset — from a TikTok clip
to a policy infographic to the service-delivery tracker in Section 19B — will
reinforce one central idea:

> **Kitui has resources. What it has lacked is leadership that understands how
> to use them — and the discipline to prove it did.**
