**Workstream 9 is the one Firefly operates and the one that decides the race.** Approximately
**86.4%** of Kitui residents are outside the internet-using population (KNBS 2019, Tier 1), and
Section 3.6.3 puts the reach of a purely digital operation at about **36.2%** of the ~200,000-vote
threshold. Every other workstream is built on the assumption that this one works.

**Workstream 14 is the one that makes the rest legible.** Without it the other thirteen are activity.
It is listed fourteenth because that is where the numbering put it; it is read first, in Section 1A.

**What is outside this scope.** Stated as plainly as what is inside, because a boundary a proposal will not draw is a boundary the
campaign discovers later:

*   **Publishing to Dr. Mulu's owned accounts.** Firefly briefs, approves and measures. It does not
    post, and does not hold credentials. The single exception is the offline layer in §8.10, which
    Firefly operates end to end.
*   **Replacing or restaffing the existing team.** Section 14.7 sets out how Firefly assesses and
    upskills it. Where a capability gap cannot be closed by upskilling, Firefly names the gap and
    the campaign decides — Firefly does not propose itself for the role.
*   **Volunteer organising** — recruitment, tiers, gamification and ward-champion management
    (Workstream 10). Firefly defines the metrics the leaderboard reports.
*   **The existing technology estate** — accounts, hosting, devices and the tooling already in use
    (Workstream 13, in part). Procuring a stack the campaign already has is not a service.
*   **Physical event logistics** — staging, PA systems, tents, transport and fuel. Section 14 names
    these as subcontracted vendor classes managed by the campaign.
*   **Campaign finance administration** — expenditure returns, IEBC financial compliance filing and
    the reconciliation behind them.
*   **Legal representation** — election petitions, litigation, and the data-protection and
    electoral-law review itself. Section 12.5.5 has the campaign appoint that reviewer; Firefly
    builds to their sign-off rather than substituting for it.
*   **Candidate scheduling** — tour management, diary and constituency office operations.
*   **Party-level negotiation** with Wiper organs, branch committees and the national executive.
*   **Polling agent recruitment, accreditation and payment.** Firefly builds the tracking behind
    indicator GE-03; the campaign supplies and accredits the agents.
*   **Commissioning the nomination tracking poll.** Section 3.1.3 specifies the instrument the
    campaign should insist on; commissioning it is the campaign's own act, and deliberately so — a
    pollster paid by the digital vendor measuring the digital vendor's effect is not evidence.
*   **`[CAMPAIGN DECISION REQUIRED]`** — anything further the campaign wishes placed outside scope
    before contracting. This line exists to be filled in, not to be left.

**How the levels change what runs.** The three engagement levels in Section 10.1 change the **depth of the intelligence**, not the volume
of Firefly's output: how often the presence audit is refreshed, how many wards carry reach reporting,
how often creative is tested, how much research runs, and how far the team upskilling goes. They do
not change the list above, they never move a workstream from one owner to another, and they never
change Workstream 4 — accessibility applies in full at every level and is not a line to trade away
(Section 10.1.1).

**Firefly recommends Level 2 (Standard).** Level 1 instruments only the twelve decisive wards and
leaves the recognition deficit measured at county level, which is the one place this campaign cannot
afford to be blind. Level 3 refreshes the analysis faster than a nomination sprint can act on it.
Standard is the level at which all 40 wards carry both reach reporting and an active SMS and USSD
layer — and reaching the arid belt is the whole argument.
